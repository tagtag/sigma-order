\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Recent work~\cite{sagawa2026} places a thermodynamic reading on a simple
measurement.  Take a fixed text $x$ of $L$ tokens, reverse its own
order to obtain $\tilde{x}$, score both with a language model, and form
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; \frac{\log P(x) - \log P(\tilde{x})}{L} .
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
Here $P$ is the joint distribution over token sequences that the evaluator
--- the model that scores the text --- defines; for an autoregressive model $P(x) = \prod_k p_\theta(x_k \mid
x_{<k})$, so the joint exists and Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} is a difference of two
of its log-probabilities.

Reference~\cite{sagawa2026} reads this as an entropy production, a positive
value as irreversibility carried by the text.  A forward--backward
asymmetry in language has been quantified before~\cite{arrow}, but by training
a second model on the reversed corpus and comparing the two;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} scores both directions under \emph{one} model, and
Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026} draws that distinction itself.  The reading is
supported by a contrast between two sets of generated passages: those whose
sentences stand in a causal sequence, and those whose sentences are arranged
arbitrarily.  The first set scores higher.  What $\tilde{x}$ is matters here
and is not incidental: that contrast is measured with the text's
\emph{sentence blocks} reversed, not its tokens.
Token reversal leaves the same tokens with no grammar, and
Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026} reports the token reversal to be dominated by that
syntactic artifact, coarse-graining to blocks for that reason.
Section~\ref{sec:fixedtext} measures both reversals --- tokens and blocks
--- on the same texts and gives the one fact that settles which of the two
the analysis uses from here on.  Neither we nor Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026}
measure \emph{why} the token reversal is dominated by that artifact; what is
measured is that it is.

We reproduce the analysis of Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026}.  Before any of what
follows, the statistical layer of the published analysis was recomputed independently from the released per-text
values of all $\ReproNCorpora$ corpora and agrees to within $\ReproMaxErr$,
and every
Mann--Whitney statistic and rank-biserial effect size the repository ships is
recovered.  Nothing below disputes a measured number.

\subsection*{Two questions left open, and the instrument one of them needs}

Reference~\cite{sagawa2026} names what its own measurement does not settle,
and does so three times.  What the entropy production means once it is
applied to a real language model it poses as a challenge, and it takes a
first step toward that with the experiment on externally prepared texts ---
rather than on sequences the model generated --- that supplies this paper's
corpora~\cite[Sec.~V~B]{sagawa2026}.  It says in the same section that the
labels that experiment turns on are defined by the generation prompt rather
than by a formal criterion, and that a prompt-independent definition of
causal ordering is what a more rigorous assessment would
need~\cite[Sec.~V~B]{sagawa2026}.  And what the step cannot separate it
states at the end: the block-level production mixes genuine causal
dependence with mere temporal ordering and with whatever else the ordering
carries, and separating those contributions ``remains a highly nontrivial
open problem''~\cite[Sec.~VIII]{sagawa2026}.

Two of the three are questions this paper takes up directly, and they need
different things.

The first is a question about what the entropy production of
Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026} means in its fixed-text reading: the setting in
which the text was produced by another model.  Answering it needs a
comparison whose zero holds on that text, which the model class
Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026} uses cannot supply.
Section~\ref{sec:notareversal} shows that no theorem places such a zero for
$\Delta$, and Section~\ref{sec:truesigma} constructs a comparison that has
one and measures it.

The third of the three, about $\Delta$, is answered in part by a different
measurement without being settled.  The distance $\Delta$ reports can be
separated into the part any arrangement a passage came in earns and the
part the labels earn, once there is an origin to measure the distance from.
Section~\ref{sec:blkord} supplies that origin and Section~\ref{sec:causal}
reads the separation off it.  No answer to the first question comes from
there, and none to the third from Section~\ref{sec:truesigma}.

The second --- the labelling --- conditions what either answer can mean,
and Section~\ref{sec:construct} weighs it separately.

The difficulty is structural rather than technical.  An autoregressive model
generates in one direction, and that direction is the text's own; its
generation order and its token order are the same object.  There is no handle
by which one could be held fixed while the other varies, so no experiment
within that model class can tell the two apart.

A masked model supplies the missing handle.  It conditions on context in any
direction, so the order in which a text is assembled becomes a parameter
independent of the text itself.  Fixing an unmasking order $\pi$, the
any-order chain rule
\begin{equation}
\log P_\pi(x) \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{L}
   \log p_\theta\!\left( x_{\pi(t)} \,\middle|\, x_{\pi(1)}, \ldots,
                         x_{\pi(t-1)} \right)
\label{eq:aoar}
\end{equation}
is a genuine log-probability, normalised over sequences.  Taking $\pi$ to be
the left-to-right and then the right-to-left order of one text gives the
two assembly orders of that text under a single model:
\begin{equation}
\sigma(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{L}
   \bigl[ \log P_{\mathrm{l2r}}(x) - \log P_{\mathrm{r2l}}(x) \bigr].
\label{eq:fb}
\end{equation}
This is not a trajectory weighed against its own reversal in the sense
of~\cite{jarzynski1997, crooks1999, seifert2012}.  Both orders run from
the empty sequence to the same completed text, so neither is the time
reverse of the other, and we do not claim Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb} as an entropy
production.  Nothing below needs it to be one: Eq.~\eqref{eq:fbzero}
follows from the any-order chain rule alone.  The quantity here that is an
entropy production is $\sigEP$ of Appendix~\ref{sec:toy}, whose chain has
a time reversal.

Equation~\eqref{eq:fb} is the comparison this paper does make,
constructed for a fixed text: one text assembled under a schedule and
under the opposite schedule, with a zero that holds on that text.
That is what a masked evaluator makes possible and an autoregressive one
does not (Section~\ref{sec:notareversal}).  Measuring it answers the first
question, though not in the direction the question anticipates, and on the
way it determines what Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} was measuring.

\subsection*{What the instrument shows}

Equation~\eqref{eq:fb} carries an identity.  If the evaluator's
conditionals are those of some joint $P$, every term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:aoar} telescopes, $\log P_\pi(x) = \log P(x)$ for every
$\pi$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
\sigma(x) = 0 \qquad \text{for every } x, \text{ causal or not.}
\label{eq:fbzero}
\end{equation}
Wherever that premise holds there is no value range to read: $\sigma$ is
the same number, zero, for every text.  Three consequences follow, and they
are the content of this paper.

\emph{First}, an autoregressive evaluator has no part here.  The backward
term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb} asks each token for its probability given what
stands to its right, and a model that attends only to what precedes does not
emit it, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb} does not stand for an autoregressive
evaluator.  What Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026} computes is Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta},
whose two terms score two \emph{different} sequences, and
Section~\ref{sec:notareversal} sets out what follows from that.  We
therefore write it $\Delta$ throughout and reserve $\sigma$ for
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb}.

\emph{Second}, $\Delta$ does separate the two label groups, and we ask what in them it responds to.  On a fixed text
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} lacks an origin.  The distribution of $\Delta$ over the
$n!$ arrangements of a text's own $n$ sentences supplies an origin, and the
authored arrangement can be located against that distribution.  Locating it
decomposes the measured distance --- how far that position lies from the
middle of the distribution --- into two: the part the order a passage came
in earns on its own, and the part only causal order earns.  The causal part
is small --- within a few multiples of the smallest non-zero value that can
be read off at all.  Read as the median of each label group's
position, what is established is that the two label groups differ, not by
how much.  Read higher in the distribution the same contrast behaves
differently.  One contrast, two statistics, two answers --- which is the
subject of Sections~\ref{sec:resolution} and~\ref{sec:window}.

\emph{Third}, $\sigma$ does not measure causality either, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fbzero} says why before any measurement is taken.  A non-zero
reading is a measure of the evaluator's \emph{in}compatibility --- its
conditionals deriving from no joint --- as exposed by that text.  We
measure it on masked evaluators, and whatever comes out is a contrast in how
much of the evaluator's defect a text exposes, which is not a property of
the text's causal structure.

The first and the third of Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026}'s three open points
therefore have answers here, and they rest on different things.

\emph{The open point about $\Delta$.}  The separation \emph{can} be carried
out on $\Delta$,
and the answer is a measurement: part of the distance is attributable to
causal order and the rest to the arrangement being the order the passage was
produced in, with the causal part within a few multiples of the smallest
non-zero value that can be read off and its size not resolved.  Nothing
in that is an identity, and on a fixed text no identity is available:
$\Delta$ is the log of the ratio between the probability given to a text and
that given to its reversal --- the difference of the two terms of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta}.  Nothing makes that ratio vanish as the autoregressive evaluator's parameter
count grows along the AR ladder; measured, its magnitude grows.  The reasons
it cannot answer the causal question are the size of that share and the
absence of any criterion defining the two label groups.

\emph{The first.}  It is answered in the other direction, and not by a
measurement.  Equation~\eqref{eq:fbzero} answers before anything is
measured: $\sigma$ is
identically zero wherever the conditionals derive from a joint, so whatever
it reports where they do not is a reading of that defect.  That is what the
quantity means, applied to a real language model --- a statement about the
quantity rather than about these corpora, and what follows confirms rather
than establishes it.

\subsection*{The labels}

The labelling is a question about the corpora rather than about the
quantity, and Ref.~\cite{sagawa2026} raises it about its own corpora before
we do~\cite[Sec.~V~B]{sagawa2026}.  The two label groups were produced by
different generation prompts, and ``causal'' there means that a
language model, asked for a passage whose sentences stand in a causal
sequence, produced one.  No independent criterion is applied --- no
intervention, no counterfactual, no specified causal structure the text is
checked against.  The sets also differ in ways other than the one of
interest: their lengths are not controlled, and the non-causal passages are not incoherent --- their topic
continues, their pronouns resolve backwards, their connectives are in place.
They are coherent discourse whose sentence order is simply not causal.

Every measurement below that bears on causal order is therefore made
\emph{within} a text, across the arrangements of its own sentences, where
length, vocabulary, topic and style are identical by construction and the
labels enter only as a grouping of the resulting ranks.  That grouping is
still a use of them, and an asymmetric one --- the decomposition of
Section~\ref{sec:decomp} is defined against the non-causal group and its
share changes sign if the two are exchanged --- so every figure derived from it
is reported here as conditional on the labelling, and
Section~\ref{sec:construct} is the separate question of what the labelling
records.
