# Terms this paper settled on, and the ones that must not come back.
#
# Every line here was a real correction, and every one of them was caught by
# a person reading the text rather than by anything mechanical.  Writing the
# decision down is what makes it checkable: a choice that lives only in the
# prose cannot be enforced, and a near-synonym reintroduced three revisions
# later reads perfectly well in its own sentence.
#
# Format, one rule per line:
#
#     preferred term | banned alternative, another one   # why
#
# check_paper.py fails if a banned alternative appears anywhere in the body.
# Matching ignores case and requires whole words.  Section headings are
# searched too, because that is where one of these drifted.

fluctuation theorem | fluctuation relation, fluctuation relations   # Sagawa uses "theorem" 19x and "relation" 2x; match the paper being re-examined
autoregressive, AR | causal evaluator, causal model, causal evaluators, causal attention, causal LM, causal language model, causal transformer   # "causal" in this paper means the TEXT label, never the attention pattern; the rule was here and the list was short, so "causal attention" survived in two places
unadjusted | naive   # the count that does not split ties is unadjusted, not naive
masked language model | bidirectional model, bidirectional evaluator   # one name for the model class, the one its own literature uses
authored order | written order   # one object, one name; the two were used interchangeably for 30 revisions and, asked what the difference was, this paper's draft invented one
permutation set, midrank | permutation construction, permutation test, permutation null   # two steps, not one: the SET supplies the origin (Eq. 6 centres it), the MIDRANK disposes of the unit (a rank is invariant under monotone rescaling).  One name for both let the set be credited with removing the scale in three places
[tex] decomposition | split, splits   # one operation, one name.  "split" also meant cutting a text into blocks and sharing tied ranks, so one word stood for three things
[tex] share | causal share   # a share divides by the TOTAL (authored + causal).  The per-cell quantity that divides by the authored component alone is a ratio and is named CausalRatio*; the two stood side by side in the abstract as 10-19% and -8% to +40%
[tex] different sequence | different text, another text   # Delta's two terms are x and its reversal, and the paper described that pair two ways: "a text weighed against a different text" where the case against the thermodynamic reading needed them distinct, and "the comparison is within each text" where the control argument needed them identical.  Both are true of one pair; "sequence" keeps the distinctness claim at the level where it holds (token sequences) and leaves "text" free to mean the material, which is what is shared
[tex] produced, came in | a writer chose, a writer settled, someone settled, the writer chose   # every passage in these corpora was generated by a language model from a prompt (Section 5.1), so "authored order" names the order a text came in, not an act of authorship.  The wording was the small half: calling it a writer's choice is also what kept the paper from asking whether the authored component reports an autoregressive sampler's output rather than anyone's decision
[tex] (nothing) | an earlier version, earlier versions, an earlier draft, earlier drafts   # a submission is not a changelog.  Twelve sentences in the body once recorded slips in unpublished drafts; what a reader needs is the claim that stands, and -- where measurements exist and are not reported -- that it is withdrawn and why.  The history belongs in the repository README, which has a section for it
#
# The terminology table, applied 2026-11-12.  One object, one word.  Four of
# these words keep a second sense and the rules are written so that sense
# survives: "position" is a token index, "step" is the 1/n! quantum, "set"
# is a set of texts, and "order" in "causal order" / "sentence order" is an
# attribute of a passage rather than one of its arrangements.
[tex] authored arrangement | authored order, written order, block order   # the arrangement a text is actually in.  Five names for it, 24+18+5+1 uses.  EXCEPTION: the title contrasts "authored order" with "causal order" as attributes, which is the same sense as "sentence order" and is kept
[tex] midrank | median position   # where a value falls within its permutation set.  "position" keeps its other sense, a token index, which is why this rule names the phrases rather than the word.  "percentile" is NOT the same case and is not listed here: a banned-form list cannot close over it, because the midrank can be called a percentile with any preposition or with a possessive, and this paper writes possessives throughout.  check_percentile_sense() fixes the permitted spelling instead -- an ordinal, "<number>th percentile" -- and fails everything else.  The SENSE that spelling is permitted FOR is a quantile of a distribution of values (Section 5.4's tail statistic, and tab_tail's header), never a position within a permutation set; "sits at the 85th percentile" passes the spelling check and is still wrong, which is why this note states the sense and not the string.  Stated positively, the permitted referent is a quantile of a distribution of VALUES -- the tail statistic reads a fraction of causal texts above a quantile of the non-causal group's own z distribution -- so a new occurrence is judged by asking whether it names one of those, not by matching the spelling
[tex] distance from the null | displacement   # one distance, one name; "displacement" was a third name used only in Section 6
[tex] grid step | quantum   # the 1/n! step of the midrank
[tex] stage | (see note)   # a step of a two-stage construction is a STAGE; "step" is reserved for the grid.  This collision was introduced when the permutation set and the midrank were separated, and there is no banned word here because "step" is legitimate in its own sense
[tex] permutation set | permutation range, permutation construction   # the n! arrangements of one text
[tex] label groups | the two sets, the two text sets   # the causal and non-causal groups.  "text set" in the general sense (a corpus built some way) is not this
